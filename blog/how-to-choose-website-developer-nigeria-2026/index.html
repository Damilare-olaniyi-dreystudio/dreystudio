How to Choose a Website Developer in Nigeria: 10 Things to Check Before Hiring

Hiring a website developer is about more than finding someone who can build a good-looking site. Here are 10 practical things to check before you choose who gets the project.

By Drey Studio · Published September 5, 2026 · Updated September 5, 2026

On this page ↓

Choosing a website developer in Nigeria is a buying decision, not just a design decision. A website project can involve strategy, content, interface design, development, mobile responsiveness, SEO, domain and hosting setup, payments, integrations, maintenance and future updates. A clear website development service scope should explain how those pieces fit together.

That is why two people can both promise to build a website while offering very different outcomes. Before you compare naira figures, understand what your business needs the website to do. The right developer is not necessarily the cheapest or the most expensive. The better fit is the person or team whose skills, process, communication, scope and support match the project.

Quick answer: Before hiring a website developer, check relevant live work, their understanding of your goals, the written scope, total pricing, ownership terms, mobile performance, SEO knowledge, communication process, post-launch support and references. Before You Hire a Website Developer

  • Review real portfolio work
  • Ask how they approach your business goals
  • Get the scope in writing
  • Understand the total cost
  • Confirm domain and hosting ownership
  • Ask about mobile performance
  • Ask what SEO is included
  • Clarify revisions
  • Clarify post-launch support
  • Check testimonials and references

1. Check Their Actual Portfolio

A portfolio is useful only when it helps you judge the developer's real work. Look beyond a polished homepage screenshot. If possible, open live websites and test them on a phone. Check whether the navigation is clear, pages load reasonably, text is readable, forms work, and the visual hierarchy makes sense.

Look for projects with a similar level of complexity to yours. A developer who built a brochure website may not automatically have experience with product catalogues, payments, customer accounts or custom workflows. Ask what the developer personally handled and what was supplied by another designer, agency or platform.

Review Drey Studio's portfolio of website projects as one example of the kind of live work a prospective client can inspect. The broader lesson is to assess evidence, not just claims.

2. Check Whether They Understand Your Business Goal

There is a major difference between saying, "I need a website," and saying, "I need a website that helps potential customers understand my services and contact me." Your goal could be lead generation, bookings, online sales, credibility, local discovery, a portfolio, customer information or an internal workflow.

A good developer should ask questions before proposing pages and features. Who is the audience? What action should a visitor take? What information do customers repeatedly request on WhatsApp? What does the business need to manage after launch?

If the conversation begins and ends with colours and animations, ask how the website will support the business. A useful website development process connects content, user journeys and implementation rather than treating the website as a poster.

3. Ask Exactly What Is Included in the Price

Website quotes are difficult to compare when the scope is vague. One quote may include custom design, content support, responsive implementation, analytics and a handover. Another may cover only a template, a few pages and basic setup.

Ask whether the quote includes design, development, mobile implementation, domain, hosting, content upload, SEO setup, contact forms, payment integration, CMS configuration, analytics, revisions, maintenance, training and post-launch support. Ask who supplies the copy, images and product data.

For more context, compare the scope against our guide to website costs in Nigeria in 2026. The point is not to force every project into a price band. It is to understand what you are actually buying.

4. Compare Scope, Not Just the Cheapest Price

A low quote is not automatically bad, and a high quote is not automatically good. The useful comparison is what each price includes: quality, functionality, performance, communication, timeline, revisions, support, ownership and likely long-term costs.

For example, a low initial figure may exclude content upload, hosting renewals, payment integration or support after launch. A higher figure may include more discovery and testing, but that still needs to be explained. Ask for assumptions and exclusions in writing, then compare like with like.

5. Make Sure You Own Your Domain and Website

Before work starts, clarify who controls the assets connected to your business. This can include the domain registration, hosting account, CMS or admin access, website files, source files where applicable, business email configuration, analytics and Search Console accounts.

The practical goal is continuity. You should not unknowingly become dependent on one person for access to your own business assets. Ask how access will be handed over, whether accounts use your business email, and what happens if you later need another developer.

Ownership arrangements vary by project, so document the agreement and ask questions before paying. This is practical buyer guidance, not legal advice.

6. Check Their Mobile Performance and SEO Knowledge

A website that looks good on a desktop monitor can still be difficult to use on a Nigerian customer's phone. Test the live portfolio on different screen sizes. Look at text size, menus, forms, tap targets, images, layout shifts and how quickly the important content appears.

Ask how the developer handles responsive design, semantic HTML, metadata, headings, image optimisation, crawlability, structured data where appropriate and basic technical SEO. Development and SEO are related, but they are not identical services. A developer should be clear about what is included and what requires an ongoing SEO plan.

Where search visibility is an important part of the project, review Drey Studio's SEO and search optimization service as an example of a separate service scope. No responsible developer should promise guaranteed Google rankings.

7. Ask How They Handle Maintenance After Launch

A website is not necessarily finished forever when it goes live. Ask who fixes bugs, updates content, handles security or hosting issues, and responds when a form or integration stops working.

Separate launch work, warranty or bug fixes, ongoing maintenance and new feature development. Ask whether maintenance is included for a defined period, what happens after that period, how future changes are estimated and who is responsible for renewals.

Do not assume that a developer's silence means support is included. A short written support arrangement is more useful than an informal promise.

8. Pay Attention to Communication and Process

Communication problems can damage an otherwise capable project. A professional process should make clear what happens first, who supplies content, when designs are reviewed, how revisions work, how progress is shared, the expected timeline and what happens when requirements change.

Ask how often you will receive updates and where feedback should be recorded. If you prefer WhatsApp for quick communication, confirm how important approvals and decisions will be documented. A technically skilled developer can still be a poor fit if communication is unreliable.

9. Check Testimonials and Real Client Work

Testimonials are more useful when they are specific and connected to identifiable projects. Look for recent feedback that describes the problem, the working relationship and the outcome without making implausible promises.

Be cautious with anonymous praise, vague superlatives, copied wording, unverifiable claims and screenshots that cannot be connected to a real website. Ask whether you can speak with a previous client if the project is significant. You do not need a long list of reviews; you need enough evidence to judge reliability.

10. Watch for Red Flags Before You Pay

Red flags are not proof that a developer cannot deliver, but they are reasons to slow down and ask better questions.

  1. They cannot show real examples of their work.
  2. They cannot explain what you are paying for.
  3. They avoid discussing ownership or access.
  4. They promise guaranteed Google rankings.
  5. They refuse to explain the development process.
  6. They keep adding unexpected costs.
  7. They have no clear communication process.
  8. They pressure you to pay everything without clear terms.
  9. They cannot explain what happens after launch.
  10. They focus only on appearance and ignore usability, mobile performance or business goals.

Questions to Ask a Website Developer Before Hiring

  1. Can I see websites you have actually built?
  2. Have you worked with businesses similar to mine?
  3. What exactly is included in your quote?
  4. Who owns the domain and hosting account?
  5. Will I receive admin access?
  6. Is mobile responsiveness included?
  7. What SEO work is included?
  8. How many revisions are included?
  9. What is the expected timeline?
  10. What happens if the project scope changes?
  11. What support do you provide after launch?
  12. What happens if I move the website to another developer later?

Freelancer vs Web Development Agency

A freelancer may offer direct communication, personal involvement, flexibility and potentially lower overhead. The limitations can include availability, capacity, single-person dependency and fewer specialist skills for complex work.

An agency may provide multiple specialists, broader capabilities, a structured process and more capacity for larger projects. Potential limitations can include higher costs, more layers of communication and less direct access to the person doing each task.

Neither option is automatically better. Choose based on project complexity, budget, required expertise, communication preference, timeline and long-term support needs.

A Simple Website Developer Hiring Checklist

Before hiring, can you answer yes to most of these?

  • I have seen their actual work.
  • I understand what they will build.
  • I understand the total cost.
  • The project scope is written down.
  • I know who owns the domain.
  • I know who controls hosting.
  • I know what SEO is included.
  • I know how revisions work.
  • I understand the timeline.
  • I know what happens after launch.
  • Communication is clear.
  • I trust their process.

If most answers are yes, you probably have enough information to make an informed decision. If several answers are unclear, ask questions before paying.

Choose the Developer That Fits Your Business

The right website developer is not simply the person with the cheapest quote or the most impressive visual design. Look for someone who understands your business, users, goals, technical requirements, budget and long-term needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I choose a good website developer in Nigeria?

Review relevant live work, ask how the developer understands your business goals, compare a written scope, confirm ownership and access, test mobile quality, discuss SEO and support, and check references before paying.

What should I ask a website developer before hiring them?

Ask what is included in the quote, who owns the domain and hosting, whether you receive admin access, how revisions work, what SEO is included, the expected timeline, and what support continues after launch.

How much does a website developer cost in Nigeria?

There is no single price. Cost depends on website type, pages, design, functionality, CMS, integrations, content, SEO, and support. Compare the written scope rather than the headline figure.

What should be included in a website development quote?

A useful quote should describe the scope, pages, features, design approach, mobile implementation, SEO deliverables, timeline, revisions, ownership, support, payment schedule, and additional costs.

Should I hire a freelancer or a web development agency?

Either can work. Consider project complexity, budget, required expertise, communication preference, delivery capacity, timeline, and long-term support rather than assuming one option is always better.

Who should own my website domain and hosting?

The business should understand and control its domain, hosting account, website access, analytics, and relevant source or content files. Confirm the access and ownership arrangement in writing before work begins.

Should a website developer also handle SEO?

A developer should understand basic technical SEO, but development and ongoing SEO are not identical services. Ask which technical and content deliverables are included and what requires separate work.

How long does it take to build a business website?

Timing depends on scope, content readiness, design rounds, functionality, integrations, approvals, and the developer's capacity. Ask for milestones and dependencies instead of relying on a universal timeline.
