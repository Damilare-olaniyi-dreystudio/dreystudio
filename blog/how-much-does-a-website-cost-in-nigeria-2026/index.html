How Much Does a Website Cost in Nigeria in 2026?

A practical guide to what different website budgets can cover, what you are actually paying for, and how to compare a quote before you commit.

If you've asked a few Nigerian developers how much it costs to build a website, you've probably noticed something strange.

One person says ₦100,000.

Another says ₦300,000.

Someone else says ₦1 million.

And all of them might be telling the truth.

The reason is simple: “a website” isn't one product.

A one-page website for a small business is very different from a 15-page company website, an online store with payments, or a custom web application.

So instead of giving you one random number, this guide explains what you can realistically expect at different budgets, what you're actually paying for, and what you should check before accepting a website quote. Quick answer: how much does a website cost in Nigeria?

As a practical 2026 budgeting guide, you can think about website projects roughly like this:

Website budgetWhat it can realistically cover
₦50,000 – ₦150,000Very basic or starter website
₦150,000 – ₦300,000Small-business website
₦300,000 – ₦700,000More polished/custom business website
₦700,000 – ₦1.5 millionAdvanced business or smaller e-commerce projects
₦1.5 million+Complex e-commerce, portals, web applications and custom systems

These aren't official fixed Nigerian prices. They are budgeting bands.

Current Nigerian providers publish significantly different ranges. For example, recent 2026 guides put standard business websites anywhere from roughly ₦150,000–₦400,000 at the lower end to ₦350,000–₦1.5 million or more for larger professional sites. Custom applications can move into several millions of naira.

The important part is not the number by itself.

It's what the developer is promising to deliver for that number.

Why do website prices vary so much in Nigeria?

Imagine two businesses.

Business A needs:

  • A homepage
  • About page
  • Services
  • Contact page
  • WhatsApp button

Business B needs:

  • 15+ pages
  • Custom UI/UX
  • Blog
  • Customer accounts
  • Product catalogue
  • Online payments
  • Order management
  • SEO setup
  • Analytics
  • Third-party integrations

Calling both projects “a website” doesn't make them the same project.

Number of pages

A five-page website requires less design, development and content work than a 20-page website.

Template vs custom design

A template can reduce design time. A custom website takes more work because the design, components and user experience are created around the business rather than simply adapted from an existing layout.

Features

Contact forms are relatively simple. Payments, booking systems, customer accounts, dashboards, inventory management and external API integrations require considerably more development and testing.

Content

If you already have your copy, images and branding ready, development can move faster. If the developer also needs to write your content, source images, create graphics or restructure your information, that's additional work.

SEO and performance

A website that simply exists is different from one that has been structured properly for search engines, mobile users and performance. Drey Studio’s SEO and AI Search Optimization service covers this kind of search-friendly foundation.

Support

Some developers disappear after handing over the website. Others include maintenance, updates or post-launch support. That difference should be reflected in the quote.

What can you realistically get for ₦50,000–₦150,000?

At this level, you're generally looking at a very simple online presence.

It could make sense for:

  • A personal brand
  • A small service
  • An event
  • A simple landing page
  • A new business testing an idea

You might get:

  • One page or a few simple pages
  • Basic responsive design
  • Contact information
  • WhatsApp button
  • Contact form
  • Basic hosting/deployment

But don't expect this budget to automatically include custom design, extensive content creation, advanced SEO, complex functionality or ongoing maintenance.

And this is important:

A cheap website isn't automatically a bad website.

If your requirements are genuinely small, spending ₦500,000 on features you don't need doesn't make sense. The problem starts when someone sells you a ₦100,000 website while promising the scope of a ₦500,000 project.

What can you get for ₦150,000–₦300,000?

This is where a more typical small-business website starts becoming realistic.

You could have:

  • Home
  • About
  • Services
  • Contact
  • FAQ
  • Portfolio or gallery
  • Mobile-responsive layouts
  • Contact/lead forms
  • WhatsApp integration
  • Basic on-page SEO
  • Google Analytics or similar tracking

The exact scope still depends on the developer.

A current 2026 Nigerian pricing guide from Zozzah places basic 3–5 page business websites around ₦150,000–₦400,000, while other current guides put small-business sites at wider ranges.

So if someone quotes ₦250,000, don't immediately ask:

“Why is yours expensive?”

Ask:

“What exactly is included?”

That's the better question.

What changes around ₦300,000–₦700,000?

Now you're entering the territory where a business can expect a more deliberate website rather than simply “some pages online.”

Depending on the project, this could include:

  • Customised UI/UX
  • More pages
  • Better mobile experience
  • Stronger visual branding
  • Blog functionality
  • Lead-generation forms
  • Better SEO foundations
  • Analytics
  • WhatsApp integration
  • Performance optimisation
  • More detailed testing
  • More revisions
  • Better post-launch support

This doesn't mean every ₦500,000 website is better than every ₦200,000 website. It means the potential scope is larger.

Two developers could both quote ₦500,000 and deliver completely different websites. One could be adapting a template. Another could be designing and developing the entire experience from scratch. The price alone won't tell you which one you're getting.

What about ₦700,000–₦1.5 million?

At this level, you're more likely to see projects with larger scope or more advanced requirements.

Examples include:

  • Larger corporate websites
  • More extensive custom design
  • E-commerce stores
  • Booking systems
  • Payment integrations
  • Product catalogues
  • Advanced forms
  • Third-party integrations
  • More detailed SEO work
  • Custom content structures

Current 2026 Nigerian guides place e-commerce and larger professional websites across a very broad range, often starting in the hundreds of thousands and reaching several million depending on complexity.

At this point, the project should really be priced from a written scope, not from a vague statement like “I need a website.”

What about websites costing ₦1.5 million, ₦2 million or more?

At some point, you're no longer talking about a normal brochure website. You may be paying for software.

Think about:

  • Customer dashboards
  • User accounts
  • Multiple user roles
  • Custom booking systems
  • Complex payment flows
  • Inventory systems
  • Admin dashboards
  • CRM integrations
  • APIs
  • SaaS functionality
  • Custom business workflows

A recent Nigerian 2026 guide, for example, places custom web applications from around ₦1.5 million upward and notes that complex applications can reach ₦20 million or more.

This is why asking “How much does a website cost?” can sometimes be the wrong question. If you're building software for your business, you need to discuss requirements, functionality and engineering time, not just the number of pages.

There can also be:

Domain

Your domain is the address people use to find your website. The price depends on the extension and registrar, so don't assume every domain costs the same amount.

Hosting

Your website needs somewhere to run. Basic shared hosting can be relatively inexpensive, while higher-traffic or more demanding websites can require more expensive infrastructure.

Business email

You may want addresses such as hello@yourbusiness.com rather than using a personal Gmail address. Depending on the setup, professional email can have its own recurring cost.

Payment processing

If your website accepts payments, the payment gateway may charge transaction fees. For example, Paystack currently lists Nigerian local transactions at 1.5% + ₦100, with the ₦100 fee waived for transactions below ₦2,500 and local transaction fees capped at ₦2,000. That is a payment processing cost, not automatically a website development cost.

Maintenance

You may also need to budget for:

  • Updates
  • Security
  • Backups
  • Content changes
  • Technical support
  • Fixes
  • Monitoring

Some websites barely need ongoing work. Others need regular attention.

What should be included in a professional website quote?

Before paying a developer, ask them to put the scope in writing.

At minimum, check:

Website structure

  • How many pages?
  • Which pages?
  • Are additional pages charged separately?

Design

  • Is the design custom?
  • Is a template being used?
  • How many design revisions are included?

Development

  • Is the website responsive?
  • Which browsers/devices are tested?
  • Is the website optimised for mobile?

Content

  • Who provides the text?
  • Who provides images?
  • Is copywriting included?

SEO

  • Are page titles and descriptions included?
  • Is the site structured properly for search engines?
  • Is an XML sitemap included?
  • Is Google Search Console setup included?

Integrations

  • WhatsApp?
  • Email?
  • Payment gateway?
  • Booking system?
  • Social media?
  • CRM?
  • Other APIs?

Launch

  • Who handles deployment?
  • Is SSL included?
  • Is the domain connected?
  • Is the website tested before launch?

Ownership

This one is important. Ask:

  • Who owns the domain?
  • Who owns the hosting account?
  • Will I receive access?
  • Will I receive the source files/code where applicable?
  • Can another developer maintain the website later?

If you don't know the answer to these questions, don't just look at the price.

Freelancer vs agency vs building it yourself

There isn't one option that's automatically best.

Freelancer

Usually a good option when:

  • The project is relatively straightforward
  • You have a limited budget
  • You can communicate directly with the person building it

The tradeoff is that one person may be handling design, development, communication and support.

Agency

Can make more sense when:

  • The project is larger
  • Multiple specialists are needed
  • You need ongoing support
  • There are multiple stakeholders

The tradeoff is usually a higher cost.

DIY

If you have the time and the project is simple, building it yourself can make sense. Website builders and modern development tools have made this much easier.

But your time has a cost too. If you spend three months learning something that a professional could build in a few weeks, the “free” option wasn't necessarily free.

When should you spend more on a website?

Spend more when the website is genuinely important to how your business operates.

For example:

If your website is simply an online business card, you probably don't need a ₦2 million build.

But if your website is expected to:

  • Generate leads
  • Sell products
  • Accept payments
  • Handle bookings
  • Manage customers
  • Rank for competitive searches
  • Connect to other systems

then the cost can reasonably increase because there's more work involved.

The goal isn't to get the cheapest website. The goal is to get the right website for what you're trying to accomplish.

So, how much should you budget?

If you're a Nigerian small business owner and you're just trying to figure out where to start, think about your requirements first.

Need a simple online presence?

Look around the lower end of the market.

Need a proper small-business website?

A budget around ₦150,000–₦300,000+ can be a reasonable starting point depending on scope.

Need a more custom professional website?

Expect the budget to move toward ₦300,000–₦700,000+ depending on the design, pages and functionality.

Need e-commerce or advanced functionality?

You may be looking at ₦700,000 to several million naira, depending heavily on what the system needs to do.

Need a custom web application?

Don't price it like a normal website. Start with the requirements and let the developer estimate the engineering work.

These are planning ranges, not guarantees or fixed industry prices. Current Nigerian providers publish substantially different figures, which reinforces the importance of comparing scope rather than relying on a single “market price.”

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a basic website cost in Nigeria in 2026?

A very basic website can start around the lower end of the market, while a more complete small-business website can cost several hundred thousand naira. The exact price depends on the number of pages, design, functionality and what's included.

Is ₦100,000 enough to build a website?

It can be enough for a simple website or landing page with limited requirements. It is unlikely to cover a large custom website with extensive functionality.

Is a ₦500,000 website expensive?

Not necessarily. ₦500,000 could be expensive for a simple template website, but reasonable for a larger custom website with more pages, design work, integrations, SEO and testing.

Do I have to pay for hosting and a domain separately?

Not always. Some developers include the first year or bundle these costs into their package. Others charge separately. Always ask what's included and what you'll need to renew yourself.

How much does an e-commerce website cost in Nigeria?

E-commerce pricing varies widely because product catalogues, checkout, payment integration, inventory, delivery and other requirements can dramatically change the workload. Current Nigerian 2026 guides commonly place e-commerce projects from hundreds of thousands into the millions depending on complexity.

What should I ask a web developer before paying?

Ask for a written scope covering pages, features, design, content, SEO, integrations, revisions, hosting, domain, launch, ownership and post-launch support.

Final thought

The biggest mistake you can make when buying a website isn't necessarily choosing the cheapest developer.

It's paying for something without understanding what you're getting.

A ₦150,000 website can be exactly what one business needs.

A ₦1 million website can be completely justified for another.

And sometimes the opposite is true.

So before asking:

“How much does a website cost in Nigeria?”

ask:

“What does my business actually need the website to do?”
